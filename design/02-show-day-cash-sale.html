Cash sale — how the screen works

Function before looks. Plain treatment on purpose. Every zone is numbered and explained: what it does, and why it exists. Reference class is Square / Shopify POS, because that is the register your friend has already stood behind.

  1. Sale header — what is open right now

    Names the sale type and counts what is in it. Void sale throws the whole thing away, and it is the only destructive control on the screen, so it sits alone and is the only red text up here.

    Why: a dealer gets interrupted mid-sale constantly. The count is how you check nothing got added while you looked away.

  2. The sale — cards already added

    One row per card: sticker number, what it is, your price, and the solds link. ✕ removes that card from the sale and does nothing else.

    Why: correcting a mis-typed number is the most common action at a table, and it was missing entirely from the last version. Your price is bold and black; the solds are grey and underlined as a link — a buyer can tell at a glance which number is your claim and which is evidence.

  3. Entry — the number you are typing

    Shows the digits as they land, with a caret. The moment they match a card in your case, the name and your price appear underneath.

    Why: this is the typo guard from the spec, and it had no home on screen before. You read the name off the phone, glance at the card in your hand, and you know you pulled the right one before it joins the sale.

  4. Keypad — and the one control that is not part of it

    Everything inside the grey grid changes the digits: the numbers, Clear wipes them, ⌫ drops one. The outlined button below the grid changes the sale.

    Why: this is the thing you caught. "Add" and "Delete" sat side by side looking identical while doing completely different jobs. Now position tells you scope — inside the pad edits what you are typing, outside the pad commits a card. The button also names the card it is about to add, so it is never a mystery press, and it stays disabled until a number actually matches.

  5. The deal — your price, the evidence, and the dial

    My price is what your stickers add to. Recent solds is what the market did, and it links out to the real eBay listings. The dial sets what you will actually take; the floor line says how much room is left.

    Why: the two numbers can never be mistaken for each other — different weight, different colour, and only one of them is clickable. That is the claims-versus-receipts rule from your spec made visible instead of written down.

  6. The charge button — carries the amount

    The button reads Take $142 cash, not "Mark sold". Move the dial and the number on the button moves with it.

    Why: the last version had $143 in the lines, $142 on the dial, and one button labelled "Mark sold" — no way to know what you just charged. The amount belongs on the control that commits it, which is how every register worth using does it.
