SHOWKARDZ · Step 1

Night before — scan the stack, then price it

Phone-first. The rear camera has autofocus and macro, so the phone is the scanner and the card stays flat on the table. The laptop is the big screen you watch the stack fill up on — same screens, more width, not a different design. This is where the market gets consulted and baked into your number, which is why the table never needs a solds figure.

  1. Stack declaration — pinned above the viewfinder

    Year, product and parallel for everything about to be scanned. Inverse bar so it cannot be missed while you are looking at the camera. Change ends this run and starts a new one.

    Why: this is the one thing the camera cannot read, and the one thing that fails silently. Wrong stack mislabels 200 cards and you find out at the table. It stays on screen the entire time precisely because you are not looking at the screen.

  2. Viewfinder — reads overlay the camera, they do not replace it

    Card flat on the table, phone above it. The number and name appear as two separate lines over the bottom of the live view. Count sits top-right.

    Why: your eyes stay in one place. Separating the two reads matters because they fail differently — a wrong name is cosmetic, a wrong number is financial, and you need to see which one is off at a glance. The count is here rather than on another screen because "did that one land?" is the question you ask forty times an hour.

  3. Confirmation is a full-width band, not a toast

    Green band plus the beep. States the card number it just took.

    Why: you are moving cards, glancing down for a fraction of a second. A small toast in a corner is invisible at that speed, and it disappears before you look. The band holds until the next card replaces it.

  4. Thumb zone — and Undo last is not optional

    Every control sits in the bottom third, reachable one-handed. Undo last removes the card just entered. Fix name corrects the read without rescanning.

    Why: the scan fires on its own when it gets a confident read — that is what makes it fast, and it is also why a wrong auto-capture must be one thumb-tap to reverse. Auto-capture without undo is a machine that quietly writes bad data faster than you can catch it.

  1. Stack stays pinned, position is always on screen

    Card 4 of 17 with 13 left. Pause stops the run and leaves the rest unpriced.

    Why: this is a long, boring pass. Knowing there are 13 left is the difference between finishing it and abandoning it half done.

  2. The comps link — step one, and the biggest control on the screen

    Opens real eBay sold and completed listings, built from the title composed at intake. The literal search string sits underneath it.

    Why: your locked decision — link out, no paid API, no number the app invents. It is first because looking before pricing is the whole discipline. The URL is printed so you can see what it actually searched and catch a bad query instead of trusting a blind link.

  3. Your price — typed after looking, never pre-filled

    The market read lives in your head and comes out here. Numeric keypad, 16px minimum so iOS does not sticky-zoom the field.

    Why: this is why show day needs no solds figure. This number already carries the market, and it is the only thing the app will ever claim as yours.

  4. Floor — optional and blank by default

    The least you would take on this card.

    Why: it is what makes the show-day floor warning possible, but most cards do not need one. A required field gets filled with a number you do not mean, and a floor you do not mean is worse than no floor.

  5. Skip is a real answer

    Skip leaves it unpriced and in the queue. Save and next commits and advances.

    Why: you will hit cards you cannot read the market on. Forcing a price makes you guess, and a guessed price at the table is worse than a card you know is not priced.

What going phone-first actually costs

Every tap of the comps link leaves the app. On a phone that is a real context switch, and in an installed PWA iOS can kill the process while you are reading eBay. So the price pass has to write its position and the half-typed price on every change, not on Save — come back and you land on card 4 of 17 with your typing intact. That is the third state above, and it is an acceptance item, not an implementation detail.

The laptop is not a separate design. Same two screens at width: intake puts the scanned list beside the viewfinder instead of behind a count, and the price pass can sit next to an eBay window. Nothing changes about what the controls do.

Two things I still need you to settle

The card number is on the back

The spec has the one-shot reading player name and card number, composing 2023 Panini Prizm Anthony Edwards #58. On most modern base cards the #58 is printed on the reverse — a single front-facing shot will not see it.

Options: drop the number and search on name alone; two shots per card; or type it when it matters. This changes the eBay query, so it changes how good your comps are.

Does that search actually return the right cards?

2023 Panini Prizm Anthony Edwards 58 with sold and completed filters is a guess at a good query. Probably fine for a base card. For a numbered parallel it may pull every colour, which makes comps useless in exactly the cases where the money is.

Worth testing on real cards before this is built. I can run a batch of these queries and show you what comes back.
